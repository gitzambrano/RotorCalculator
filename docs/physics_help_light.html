RotorCalculator Technical Help

This document defines the aerodynamic model used by RotorCalculator. It uses the same signs, symbols, inputs, and outputs as the application. Use it to configure a rotor, interpret results, and identify model limits.

Model summary. The model represents a rigid rotor with blade-element theory, linear section lift, constant profile drag, first-harmonic inflow, and momentum-theory closure. Lift-induced loads use analytical radial moments. Profile force and profile torque use numerical vector integration. Induced torque and air power use an energy balance with the induced power factor.

1. Coordinate System and Signs top

RotorCalculator uses one hub-fixed coordinate system for all inputs, equations, and results. +X points forward, +Y points right, and +Z points down. Rotor rotation is counter-clockwise when viewed from above. Azimuth ψ starts at the tail and increases with rotor rotation.

Flow Variables

Use μ ≡ μx for the advance ratio in the equations. Positive Vx is the in-plane relative wind from nose to tail. Positive Vz and μz are downward through the disk. This sign is also the positive climb convention used by the app.

Vx=Vcos⁡α,Vz=−Vsin⁡α,μz=−μtan⁡α

The equation converts the same flight state between airspeed components and disk angle of attack. Positive α gives an upward normal component of relative wind. Therefore Vz and μz are negative when α is positive. Select either μx or Vx for horizontal flow. Select one of α, Vz, or μz for axial flow. Do not add equivalent representations.

2. Rotor Geometry top

The model uses a straight blade with linear chord and linear pitch. The radial coordinate is x = r/R. The physical blade starts at the root cutout x0 and ends at x = 1.

c(x)=cR+(cT−cR)x,σ(x)=Nbc(x)πR=s0+s1x,x0≤x≤1

The first equation defines the reference planform. cR is the chord of the linear planform at the rotation axis. The physical blade can start outboard of that point. Local solidity follows directly from local chord and blade count.

θ(x)=θR+θtwistx−x01−x0+Δθ=t0+t1x

The second equation defines the operating pitch. θR and θtwist define the baseline linear pitch law. Collective Δθ adds the same angle at every radial station. The aerodynamic equations use radians.

3. Operating Condition top

Rotor Speed and Flow

Ω=2πrpm60,ΩR,Vx=μΩR,Vz=μzΩR,α=arctan⁡(−μzμ)

Rotor speed sets Ω and tip speed ΩR. Advance ratio and axial-flow ratio are velocities normalized by ΩR. If you enter dimensional airspeed, a change in rotor speed changes the corresponding nondimensional ratio. If you enter the ratio, RotorCalculator preserves that ratio and updates the dimensional speed.

Collective Pitch

Collective Δθ is a uniform pitch increment. RotorCalculator adds Δθ to θ(x) at every station. A trim solution can calculate Δθ when collective is not prescribed.

Atmosphere

Pressure altitude sets static pressure with the tropospheric ISA pressure law. Ambient temperature is an independent input. Density follows the ideal-gas law, and speed of sound follows the perfect-gas relation.

p=101325(1−0.0065h288.15)5.2561Pa,ρ=pRgasTamb,a∞=γRgasTamb

4. Inflow top

Mean Inflow and Momentum Closure

λ=μz+λi,λi=viΩR

Total inflow λ is the sum of free-stream axial flow and induced flow. All three quantities are positive downward.

CT=2B2λiμ2+λ2

The second equation is the Glauert momentum relation for the effective disk area B²A. RotorCalculator calculates blade-element thrust and solves λi until blade-element thrust equals momentum-theory thrust. In ideal hover with B = 1, the equation reduces to λi = √(CT/2).

First-Harmonic Inflow

λd(x,ψ)=λ+λix(Kxcos⁡ψ+Kysin⁡ψ),λ1c=Kxλi,λ1s=Kyλi

The local induced inflow contains a mean term and two first-harmonic gradients. Kx is the longitudinal gradient. Ky is the lateral gradient. λ1c and λ1s are the corresponding harmonic amplitudes.

tan⁡χ2=μμ2+λ2+|λ|

The wake-skew relation gives χ from advance ratio and total inflow. χ = 0 in axial hover. Wake skew increases as the in-plane flow becomes large relative to the through-disk flow.

ModelKxKy
Uniform (Glauert)00
Coleman simpletan(χ/2)0
Coleman-Feingold (NDARC)(15π/32)·tan(χ/2)−2μ

Uniform inflow sets both gradients to zero. Coleman, Coleman-Feingold, and Drees provide progressively more structured first-harmonic approximations. The selected gradients affect disk loading and the corresponding force and moment coefficients.

Tip Loss

ModeBEffect
None1Loading extends to the physical tip. No tip-loss correction is applied.

Induced Power Factor

The induced power factor kind changes the energy balance. It does not change the momentum closure or blade-element thrust. kind = 1 is the ideal induced-power case. Values above 1 represent additional induced losses.

5. Aerodynamic Equations top

The implemented load model uses blade-element theory with a small inflow angle. Lift uses a linear lift curve. Profile drag uses a constant Cd0. Lift-induced loads use analytical radial moments. Profile force and profile torque use numerical vector integration over radius and azimuth.

Section Kinematics

φ=arctan⁡(uPuT),αs=θ−φ,Cl=aαs

The local inflow angle φ follows from the normal and tangential velocity components. The section angle of attack is geometric pitch minus inflow angle. The linear lift law Cl = aαs supplies the sectional lift used by the analytical load equations.

Radial Moments

Linear chord and linear pitch make the radial integrations analytic. Jn is a pure radial moment. Im weights the radius with solidity. Θm weights the radius with solidity and pitch.

Jn=Bn+1−x0n+1n+1

Jn integrates xn between the root cutout and the lift-producing tip station B.

Im=s0Jm+s1Jm+1

Im substitutes the linear solidity law into the radial integral. The result contains only J moments.

Θm=s0t0Jm+(s0t1+s1t0)Jm+1+s1t1Jm+2

Θm also includes the linear pitch law. This moment carries the chord and pitch distributions into the closed-form load equations.

Thrust Coefficient CT

The blade-element normal force is averaged over azimuth and integrated over radius. The integral form shows the source of each contribution.

CT=12∫x0Bσ(x)a[θ(x)(x2+μ22)−λx−12λ1sμx]dx

Pitch produces positive thrust. Advance ratio adds the μ² pitch term. Total inflow reduces section angle of attack. The lateral inflow harmonic λ1s adds the first-harmonic correction.

CT=a2[Θ2+μ22Θ0−(λ+12μλ1s)I1]

Substitution of the radial moments gives the implemented closed form. This equation is evaluated together with the momentum equation to solve the mean induced inflow.

Induced In-Plane Force CHi

CHi=a4[μλΘ0+λ1s(Θ2−2λI1)]

Lift tilts rearward when the blade has a positive inflow angle. The rearward component produces CHi. The first term is the mean forward-flight contribution. The second term is the contribution from the lateral inflow harmonic.

Profile In-Plane Force CH0 and Profile Torque CQ0

Profile drag acts along the full local relative-velocity vector. RotorCalculator integrates this drag numerically over x0 ≤ x ≤ 1 and over rotor azimuth. The calculation includes radial velocity and axial flow.

CH0=∫x01σ(x)Cd02⟨W(xsin⁡ψ+μ)⟩ψdx

CH0 is the forward-flight component of profile drag in the disk plane.

CQ0=∫x01σ(x)Cd02⟨WuTx⟩ψdx

CQ0 is the profile-drag moment about the rotor shaft.

W=uT2+uR2+μz2,uT=x+μsin⁡ψ,uR=μcos⁡ψ

W is the nondimensional magnitude of local relative velocity. uT is its tangential component and uR is its radial component. The numerical integration uses the physical blade span, so tip-loss factor B does not truncate profile drag.

For a rectangular blade, zero axial flow, and small advance ratio, the numerical formulation approaches these low-order vectorial expressions:

CH0≈3σCd08μ,CQ0≈σCd08(1+1.5μ2)

These expressions are checks, not a separate operating model.

Total In-Plane Force and Shaft Torque

CH=CHi+CH0,CQ=CQi+CQ0=CP

Total in-plane force is the sum of induced and profile components. Shaft torque is the sum of induced and profile torque. With the standard rotor normalization, shaft power coefficient equals torque coefficient: CP,shaft = CQ.

Induced Torque CQi

CQi=kindλiCT+μzCT−μCHi

The induced torque follows from the rotor energy balance. kindλiCT is induced power. μzCT is axial climb or descent work. −μCHi removes the translational work already carried by the induced in-plane force.

Side Force and Hub Moments

CY=−a4λ1c(Θ2−2λI1)

The longitudinal inflow gradient λ1c produces side force in this fixed-hub convention.

CMx=−aμ2(Θ2−12λI1)+a4λ1sI3,CMy=a4λ1cI3

CMx contains the forward-flight roll contribution and the lateral inflow-gradient correction. CMy follows from the longitudinal inflow gradient. Because the model is rigid, these hub moments are not relieved by rotor flapping.

Air Power Coefficient CPair

CPair=kindλiCT+μzCT+CQ0+μCH0

Air power is the sum of induced power, axial work, profile torque power, and translational profile-drag work. The profile contribution relative to the air is CQ0 + μCH0.

CP0,air=CQ0+μCH0≈σCd08(1+4.5μ2)

The 4.5μ² factor is a special low-order result for profile power relative to the air. It is not the profile torque correction. With the implemented energy-balance torque, the complete air-power equation also satisfies:

CPair=CQ+μCH

This identity is an energy check. Do not add an extra μCHi term to CPair.

Prandtl-Glauert Compressibility Correction

a=a0max⁡(0.01,1−Meff2),Meff=min⁡(0.85,Mtip0.752+12μ2)

When enabled, Prandtl-Glauert increases the effective lift-curve slope with representative Mach number. RotorCalculator limits Meff in this correction to 0.85. The correction does not model shocks, wave drag, or transonic airfoil behavior.

Closed-Form Checks

For a rectangular blade with uniform pitch, no root cutout, no tip loss, and uniform inflow, the general equations reduce to the standard low-order forward-flight relations:

CT=σa2[θ(13+μ22)−λ2],CQ0=σCd08(1+1.5μ2)

The first relation is the classical thrust expression. The second is the low-order vectorial profile-torque expression. These reductions are useful verification cases for geometry and sign conventions.

Flow and Blade Diagnostics

Vz,tot=Vz+vi=λΩR,Vadv=ΩR+Vx,Vret=ΩR−Vx

Total axial speed combines free-stream axial velocity and induced velocity. Advancing and retreating speeds are signed tangential tip velocities.

Mret=|ΩR−Vx|a∞,φ75=arctan⁡(uPuT),αs,75=θ(0.75)−φ75

The 75% radius diagnostics use the local tangential and through-disk velocities at ψ = 90° and 270°. They show aerodynamic asymmetry in the rigid-blade model. They do not predict stall or blade dynamics.

7. Trim and Sweep top

Operating Constraints

The operating state contains four linked quantities: rotor speed Ω, collective Δθ, thrust coefficient CT, and thrust T. You prescribe two quantities. RotorCalculator solves the other two at the selected atmosphere and flight condition.

8. Model Scope and Limits top

Use the model inside the assumptions below. A numerically converged solution is not evidence that the physical assumptions are valid.

  • Rigid rotor: no flapping, lead-lag, cyclic feathering, elastic blade deformation, or hub articulation.
  • Linear section lift: Cl = aαs. The model does not calculate static stall, dynamic stall, or reverse-flow aerodynamics.
  • Constant profile drag: Cd0 does not change with lift coefficient, Reynolds number, radius, azimuth, or Mach number.
  • Small inflow angle: the analytical lift-induced loads use the standard small-angle blade-element reduction.
  • First-harmonic inflow: the model does not include a free-wake solution, dynamic inflow, wake roll-up, or higher inflow harmonics.
  • Momentum-theory operating state: do not use the model as a vortex-ring-state or windmill-brake-state model.
  • Tip loss: B truncates lift-producing loading and momentum area. Profile drag remains on the physical blade to x = 1.
  • Compressibility: Prandtl-Glauert changes lift-curve slope only. The model does not include shocks, drag divergence, or transonic section data.
  • Environment: no ground effect, rotor-rotor interference, airframe interference, or atmospheric humidity model.
  • Atmosphere: the pressure-altitude relation uses the ISA troposphere. Ambient temperature is an independent input.
