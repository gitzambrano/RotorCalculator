Model summary. RotorCalculator implements a semi-analytical Blade Element Theory (BET) rotor solver with linear section lift, constant profile drag, first-harmonic inflow, and momentum-theory closure. Lift-induced forces and moments are integrated in closed form via radial moments. Profile drag forces and shaft torque are evaluated by numerical quadrature over the complete relative-velocity vector field. Induced torque and air power are coupled through an energy-balance formulation incorporating the induced power factor.

1. Coordinate System and Signs top

RotorCalculator defines a single hub-centered Cartesian coordinate frame for all geometric parameters, velocity vectors, and rotor loads. The +X axis points forward along the longitudinal vehicle axis, the +Y axis points to starboard (right), and the +Z axis points downward through the rotor disk. Viewed from above, rotor rotation Ω is counter-clockwise. The blade azimuthal angle ψ is measured from the tail (aft) in the direction of rotor rotation, placing the advancing blade at ψ = 90°, the nose at ψ = 180°, and the retreating blade at ψ = 270°.

Flow Variables

In all forward-flight equations, μ denotes the advance ratio along the rotor disk plane (μ ≡ μx = Vx / ΩR). Positive Vx represents the in-plane relative wind directed from nose to tail. In accordance with rotary-wing theory, positive axial velocity Vz and axial flow ratio μz are oriented downward through the rotor disk along the positive Z-axis. Positive vehicle climb therefore creates an upward relative inflow through the disk, producing negative Vz and μz values.

Vx=Vcos⁡α,Vz=−Vsin⁡α,μz=−μtan⁡α

These kinematic expressions transform the operating flight condition between airspeed components and the rotor disk angle of attack α. A positive angle of attack tilts the rotor disk rearward into the relative wind, producing an upward through-disk velocity component; hence Vz and μz are negative whenever α is positive. RotorCalculator accepts either advance ratio μ or forward speed Vx for in-plane flow, and one of α, Vz, or μz for axial flow, automatically preserving mathematical equivalence across all representations.

2. Rotor Geometry top

RotorCalculator models a rotor system comprising Nb identical, straight blades executing steady rotation about the hub axis. The nondimensional blade radial coordinate is x = r/R, where r is the local dimensional radius and R is the rotor tip radius. Aerodynamic blade elements begin at the root cutout station x0 = rroot/R and terminate at the blade tip x = 1.

c(x)=cR+(cT−cR)x,σ(x)=Nbc(x)πR=s0+s1x,x0≤x≤1

The blade planform follows a linear chord distribution extrapolated inward to the rotational hub axis (x = 0). Here cR denotes the reference root chord at the center of rotation, and cT represents the physical tip chord at x = 1. This parameterization defines the local linear solidity σ(x) = s0 + s1x. While the mathematical reference planform extends to the hub center, aerodynamic loading is integrated strictly across the lifting blade span x0 ≤ x ≤ B.

θ(x)=θR+θtwistx−x01−x0+Δθ=t0+t1x

The operating blade pitch distribution θ(x) combines a baseline linear twist distribution with a uniform collective pitch increment Δθ. Built-in aerodynamic twist is defined by the baseline pitch at the root cutout θR and the total twist angle θtwist = θT − θR. Applying collective pitch rotates the blade rigidly about its feathering axis, imparting a uniform angular increment across all radial stations without altering the spanwise twist gradient. All aerodynamic formulations evaluate pitch angles in radians.

3. Operating Condition top

Rotor Speed and Flow

Ω=2πrpm60,ΩR,Vx=μΩR,Vz=μzΩR,α=arctan⁡(−μzμ)

Rotor rotational speed Ω sets the angular velocity and blade tip speed ΩR. The advance ratio μ ≡ μx = Vx / (ΩR) and axial flow ratio μz = Vz / (ΩR) normalize translational velocity components by tip speed. When dimensional forward airspeed is specified, adjusting rotor speed recomputes the advance ratio. Conversely, when the advance ratio is specified directly, RotorCalculator preserves kinematic similitude and scales dimensional forward speed with ΩR.

Collective Pitch

Collective pitch increment Δθ represents a rigid blade rotation about the pitch feathering axis. RotorCalculator superimposes Δθ onto the spanwise pitch distribution θ(x) uniformly across all blade sections. When solving for an operational trim state, RotorCalculator iteratively computes the required collective pitch to achieve a target thrust or thrust coefficient.

Atmosphere

Atmospheric state variables are evaluated using the standard tropospheric equations of the International Standard Atmosphere (ISA). RotorCalculator computes ambient static pressure p directly from pressure altitude h, while ambient temperature Tamb is specified independently. Local air density ρ is calculated via the ideal gas law, and the speed of sound a∞ is determined from the adiabatic relation:

p=101325(1−0.0065h288.15)5.2561Pa,ρ=pRgasTamb,a∞=γRgasTamb

4. Inflow top

Mean Inflow and Momentum Closure

λ=μz+λi,λi=viΩR

Total inflow λ is the sum of free-stream axial flow and induced flow. In accordance with rotary-wing convention, all three inflow quantities are defined positive downward through the rotor disk.

CT=2B2λiμ2+λ2

Under the actuator disk formulation developed by Glauert, momentum conservation relates rotor thrust coefficient CT to the induced inflow velocity across the effective disk area B²A. RotorCalculator couples Blade Element Theory with momentum theory by resolving λi through an internal bisection algorithm. The solver iterates until the thrust coefficient derived from sectional blade-element integration matches the thrust coefficient governed by momentum balance. In ideal hover (μ = 0, μz = 0) with B = 1, this balance reduces to the classical relation λi = √(CT/2).

First-Harmonic Inflow

λd(x,ψ)=λ+λix(Kxcos⁡ψ+Kysin⁡ψ),λ1c=Kxλi,λ1s=Kyλi

To capture spanwise and azimuthal variations in induced velocity across the disk in forward flight, RotorCalculator models non-uniform inflow using a first-harmonic planar distribution. The coefficient Kx represents the longitudinal inflow gradient (fore-to-aft variation), and Ky represents the lateral inflow gradient (advancing-to-retreating variation). The parameters λ1c and λ1s denote the respective harmonic inflow amplitudes.

tan⁡χ2=μμ2+λ2+|λ|

The wake skew angle χ characterizes the inclination of the contracted wake cylinder relative to the shaft axis. In axial hover, χ = 0. Wake skew increases continuously as translational forward speed increases relative to total through-disk flow.

Because blade tip vortices relieve bound circulation near the tip, outer blade sections experience a rapid loss of aerodynamic lift. Profile drag remains integrated across the entire physical blade span up to x = 1, ensuring that viscous drag on the blade tip is fully accounted for.

Induced Power Factor

The induced power factor kind accounts for non-ideal induced losses arising from non-uniform inflow distributions, tip vortex roll-up, and wake contraction. In RotorCalculator, kind scales induced power within the energy-balance formulation (CPi = kindλiCT) without altering the momentum-theory equilibrium between thrust and mean induced velocity.

5. Aerodynamic Equations top

RotorCalculator evaluates rotor forces and moments by synthesizing Blade Element Theory with analytical radial integration. The mathematical formulation assumes small inflow angles φ ≈ uP / uT, a linear sectional lift relationship Cl = aαs, and a constant profile drag coefficient Cd0. Lift-induced loads are resolved in closed form via analytical radial moments, whereas profile drag loads and shaft torque are evaluated by numerical vector quadrature across radius and azimuth.

Section Kinematics

φ=arctan⁡(uPuT),αs=θ−φ,Cl=aαs

At blade station x and rotor azimuth ψ, the local velocity field is decomposed into tangential, perpendicular, and radial components normalized by blade tip speed: uT = x + μ sin ψ, uP = λd(x, ψ), and uR = μ cos ψ. The sectional inflow angle is φ ≈ uP / uT, and the effective section angle of attack is αs = θ(x) − φ. The linear lift relationship Cl = aαs supplies the sectional lift utilized by analytical blade element integrations.

Radial Moments

Because blade chord σ(x) = s0 + s1x and pitch angle θ(x) = t0 + t1x are linear functions of radius, the radial integrals of sectional lift and induced forces evaluate analytically in closed form. RotorCalculator defines three families of radial moments integrated between root cutout x0 and tip-loss station B:

Jn=Bn+1−x0n+1n+1

The moment Jn integrates xn between root cutout x0 and lift-producing tip station B.

Im=s0Jm+s1Jm+1

The moment Im incorporates the linear solidity distribution into the radial integral, expressing solidity-weighted radial moments as linear combinations of J moments.

Θm=s0t0Jm+(s0t1+s1t0)Jm+1+s1t1Jm+2

The moment Θm incorporates both linear solidity and linear pitch distributions. These analytical moments enable RotorCalculator to compute rotor loads in closed form without numerical integration error.

Thrust Coefficient CT

The blade-element normal force is averaged over azimuth (0 to 2π) and integrated across the lifting span (x0 to B). The integral structure illustrates each physical mechanism contributing to net rotor thrust:

CT=12∫x0Bσ(x)a[θ(x)(x2+μ22)−λx−12λ1sμx]dx

Blade pitch generates positive thrust. The advance ratio introduces the μ² pitch contribution representing the azimuthally averaged increase in dynamic pressure. Downward inflow λ diminishes section angle of attack. The lateral inflow harmonic λ1s introduces a first-harmonic aerodynamic correction.

CT=a2[Θ2+μ22Θ0−(λ+12μλ1s)I1]

Expressing this integral in terms of radial moments yields the closed-form formulation implemented in RotorCalculator. This equation is evaluated simultaneously with momentum theory to resolve mean induced inflow.

Induced In-Plane Force CHi

CHi=a4[μλΘ0+λ1s(Θ2−2λI1)]

Sectional lift vectors tilt rearward under the influence of positive downward inflow. The resulting in-plane component produces the induced longitudinal force CHi opposing forward motion. The first term reflects momentum drag induced by mean inflow in forward flight. The second term originates from lateral inflow asymmetry interacting with rotational dynamic pressure.

Profile In-Plane Force CH0 and Profile Torque CQ0

Profile drag acts along the complete local relative-velocity vector. RotorCalculator evaluates profile drag via numerical two-dimensional Gauss-Legendre quadrature (16 radial stations × 24 azimuthal stations) over the lifting span x0 ≤ x ≤ 1 and rotor azimuth, accounting for tangential, radial, and axial flow components:

CH0=∫x01σ(x)Cd02⟨W(xsin⁡ψ+μ)⟩ψdx

The coefficient CH0 represents the longitudinal in-plane force arising from profile drag.

CQ0=∫x01σ(x)Cd02⟨WuTx⟩ψdx

The coefficient CQ0 represents the profile drag torque resisting rotor shaft rotation.

W=uT2+uR2+μz2,uT=x+μsin⁡ψ,uR=μcos⁡ψ

The variable W denotes the nondimensional magnitude of local resultant air velocity, while uT and uR denote tangential and radial velocity components. Because profile drag affects the entire blade structure, numerical integration spans the full physical blade from root cutout x0 to tip x = 1, unaffected by the tip-loss factor B.

For an untapered blade in low-speed level flight, this numerical formulation approaches the classical low-order asymptotic expressions:

CH0≈3σCd08μ,CQ0≈σCd08(1+1.5μ2)

These closed-form expressions serve as analytical benchmarks for verification.

Total In-Plane Force and Shaft Torque

CH=CHi+CH0,CQ=CQi+CQ0=CP

Total in-plane force is the sum of induced and profile components. Total shaft torque combines induced torque and profile torque. Under standard rotorcraft definitions, the dimensionless shaft power coefficient CP is identical to the shaft torque coefficient CQ.

Induced Torque CQi

CQi=kindλiCT+μzCT−μCHi

RotorCalculator determines induced shaft torque CQi from the rotor energy-balance principle. In this formulation, kindλiCT represents induced power required to produce thrust, μzCT represents potential work in climb or descent, and −μCHi represents propulsive work exchanged with vehicle translation.

Side Force and Hub Moments

CY=−a4λ1c(Θ2−2λI1)

The longitudinal inflow gradient λ1c generates an aerodynamic side force CY acting toward starboard.

CMx=−aμ2(Θ2−12λI1)+a4λ1sI3,CMy=a4λ1cI3

The roll moment CMx incorporates the forward-flight roll contribution and lateral inflow gradient correction. The pitch moment CMy originates from the longitudinal inflow gradient. Because RotorCalculator models a rigid rotor without blade flapping or cyclic pitch relief, these hub moments represent the pure aerodynamic loads exerted on a rigid rotor mast.

Air Power Coefficient CPair

CPair=kindλiCT+μzCT+CQ0+μCH0

Air power measures the total aerodynamic rate of work performed by the rotor on the fluid, combining shaft torque work and horizontal propulsive work. The profile contribution relative to the air is CQ0 + μCH0.

CP0,air=CQ0+μCH0≈σCd08(1+4.5μ2)

The factor 4.5μ² is a characteristic rotary-wing result reflecting the sum of rotational profile torque (1.5μ²) and translational profile drag work (3.0μ²). Across the complete system, the energy-balance formulation satisfies the exact identity:

CPair=CQ+μCH

This identity confirms full thermodynamic and mechanical consistency across all reported force and power quantities.

Prandtl-Glauert Compressibility Correction

a=a0max⁡(0.01,1−Meff2),Meff=min⁡(0.85,Mtip0.752+12μ2)

When enabled, the Prandtl-Glauert correction scales the sectional lift-curve slope according to a representative blade Mach number evaluated at the 75% radial station. RotorCalculator bounds Meff at 0.85 to maintain numerical regularity. The correction accounts for subsonic lift slope steepening prior to the onset of transonic shock formation.

Closed-Form Checks

For an ideal rectangular blade with uniform pitch, zero root cutout, no tip loss, and uniform inflow, the general formulation reduces to the classical forward-flight relations:

CT=σa2[θ(13+μ22)−λ2],CQ0=σCd08(1+1.5μ2)

The first relation is the classical thrust expression. The second is the low-order vectorial profile-torque expression. These reductions serve as foundational verification benchmarks for the numerical implementation.

7. Trim and Sweep top

Operating Constraints

A steady rotor operating condition is defined by four coupled parameters: rotor speed Ω, collective pitch increment Δθ, thrust coefficient CT, and dimensional thrust T. RotorCalculator enables the specification of any two independent parameters, while the internal trim algorithm resolves the complementary pair for the specified flight condition and atmospheric state.

8. Model Scope and Limits top

RotorCalculator provides rapid, high-fidelity preliminary aeromechanical estimation for conceptual sizing, parametric trade studies, and educational analysis. The solver operates within well-defined physical boundaries governed by the following engineering assumptions:

  • Rigid rotor formulation: rotor blades are modeled as rigid cantilevers without flapping hinges, lag hinges, cyclic feathering, pitch-lag couplings, or aeroelastic flexibility. Hub rolling and pitching moments are transferred directly to the hub mast without cyclic feathering or flapping relief.
  • Linear aerodynamic model: sectional lift follows the linear thin-airfoil relation Cl = aαs. The formulation does not simulate static airfoil stall, dynamic stall delays, or reverse-flow lift reversal on the retreating blade.
  • Constant profile drag: profile drag coefficient Cd0 is assumed invariant with angle of attack, Mach number, Reynolds number, or spanwise location.
  • Small inflow angle approximation: lift-induced force equations employ the small-angle trigonometric simplification sin φ ≈ φ, cos φ ≈ 1.
  • Planar first-harmonic inflow: inflow modeling is limited to mean momentum flow combined with first-harmonic linear inflow gradients. The solver does not compute free-wake vortex geometries, tip vortex roll-up, dynamic inflow transients, or higher-harmonic inflow distortions.
  • Normal working state momentum boundaries: momentum-theory closure is valid strictly within the normal working state (hover, forward flight, and axial climb). The formulation does not represent the turbulent wake state, vortex ring state, or windmill brake state.
  • Tip-loss truncation: tip loss truncates lift generation and effective disk area at radial station B, while profile drag is integrated across the full physical blade span up to x = 1.
  • Subsonic compressibility scope: the Prandtl-Glauert correction accounts for subsonic lift-curve steepening up to Meff = 0.85, without modeling transonic shock wave development, wave drag divergence, or transonic pitching moment shifts.
  • Isolated rotor system: calculations apply to an isolated rotor operating in unbounded free air, without ground effect, fuselage interference, tail rotor interaction, or atmospheric humidity modeling.
  • Tropospheric atmosphere: barometric pressure conforms to the ISA troposphere up to 11,000 m. Ambient temperature is an independent input.
