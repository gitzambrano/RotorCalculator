RotorCalculator Physics Manual

Offline reference for every quantity in the app: conventions, geometry, flight condition, inflow, the analytical coefficient equations, derived outputs, trim, and the limits of the model. All equations below are what the app computes. Symbols match the labels on screen.

Model in one paragraph. A rigid rotor (no flapping, no lead-lag, no cyclic) is described by blade element theory with a linear lift curve, a constant profile drag coefficient and a first-harmonic inflow field. The mean induced velocity closes through momentum theory. Thrust, in-plane force, side force and hub moments follow analytically from radial moments of solidity and pitch. Profile terms are integrated numerically, and the induced torque and air power follow from an energy balance with the induced power factor.

1. Conventions top

The rotor is described in a hub-fixed body frame. +X points forward (nose), +Y to the right, +Z down. The rotor turns counter-clockwise seen from above. The azimuth ψ starts at the tail (ψ = 0°) and increases in the direction of rotation: 90° is the right (advancing) side, 180° the nose, 270° the left (retreating) side.

Flow

  • Vx, μx: component of the relative wind in the disk plane, directed toward the tail. μx ≥ 0 in the app; μx = 0 is hover or pure axial flow.
  • Vz, μz: component normal to the disk, positive downward through the disk, which is climb. Negative values are descent.
  • α: disk angle of attack, positive when the stream arrives from below (disk tilted nose up). Then the flow rises through the disk, so Vz and μz are negative.

Vx=Vcos⁡α,Vz=−Vsin⁡α,μz=−μtan⁡α

Horizontal flow is entered as μx or Vx, axial flow as α, Vz or μz. They are alternative views of the same variable and are never added.

2. Geometry top

The blade is a straight linear-taper planform. The chord law is defined from the rotation axis (x = 0) to the tip (x = 1) and the aerodynamic part of the blade starts at the root cutout x0:

c(x)=cR+(cT−cR)x,σ(x)=Nbc(x)πR=s0+s1x,x0≤x≤1
θ(x)=θR+θtwistx−x01−x0+Δθ=t0+t1x

with s0=NbcR/(πR), s1=Nb(cT−cR)/(πR), t1=θtwist/(1−x0) and t0=θR−t1x0 (angles in radians inside the equations). The root chord cR is the chord of the planform extended inward to the axis. It is not the chord at the cutout.

Vx and μx are two ways to enter the same variable; so are α, Vz and μz. Changing one updates the other representations. Changing rotor speed keeps the entered representation, so a typed Vx moves μx when Ω changes.

Collective

Δθ is added to the pitch at every station (see the θ(x) law in Section 2). It is typed, or solved by trim.

4. Inflow top

Total inflow and momentum closure

The flow through the disk is the axial free-stream part plus the induced part, both positive downward and divided by tip speed:

λ=μz+λi,λi=viΩR

Momentum theory (Glauert) gives the thrust of an actuator disk with effective area B²A, valid for hover, climb and forward flight:

CT=2B2λiμ2+λ2

The blade-element thrust CT,BET(λi) (Section 5) must equal this. The app solves f(λi)=CT,BET−2B2λiμ2+λ2=0 for λi≥0 by bracketing and bisection (tolerance 10-13). If f(0)<0 no physical root exists and the result is flagged invalid. In ideal hover (μ = 0, μz = 0, B = 1) it reduces to λi = √(CT/2), the hover inflow ratio λh.

μ

Induced power factor

kind does not enter the inflow or the thrust. It is applied in the energy balance for CQi, CPair and the figure of merit (Sections 5 and 6) to account for non-uniform inflow, tip loss and swirl. 1 is the ideal rotor.

For a rectangular blade with uniform pitch θ, no cutout, no tip loss and uniform inflow, the formulas reduce to the textbook relations:

7. Trim top

Operating pairs

The operating state has four linked variables: Ω, Δθ, CT and T, tied by T=CTρA(ΩR)2. You prescribe any two and the engine solves the other two.

PrescribedSolvedHow
Ω and ΔθCT, TDirect evaluation.
Ω and CTΔθ, TCollective bisection until CT matches.
Ω and TΔθ, CTCollective bisection until T matches.
Δθ and CTΩ, TSearch for the speed that gives the target. Invalid if non-unique or absent (for example with only dimensionless flow and no Prandtl-Glauert, CT does not depend on Ω).
Δθ and TΩ, CTSearch for the speed that gives the target thrust.
CT and TΩ, ΔθΩR=T/(ρACT) directly, then collective bisection.

Sweep trim modes

The sweep varies μ and can keep the thrust target in five ways:

ModeHeld fixedRe-solved
No Trim (Fixed Controls)Δθ and Ω from ConditionsNothing; thrust varies with μ.
Trim Collective Δθ · Every PointΩΔθ at every μ so the target holds.
Trim Rotor Speed Ω · Every PointΔθΩ at every μ so the target holds.

The target is the one set on Conditions (CT or T). The sweep X axis can be μx or Vx; each point is computed in μx and Vx follows through the tip speed of that curve.

8. Model scope and limitations top

  • Rigid hub. No flapping, lead-lag or cyclic pitch. The in-plane force, side force and hub moments are the full loads transferred to the mast. Articulated rotors would relieve them by disk tilt.
  • Linear aerodynamics. Cl = aαe with no stall, no dynamic stall and no reverse-flow region. Be cautious above μ ≈ 0.4 and check C̄L against the section Cl,max.
  • Constant drag. Cd0 is one mean value; it does not follow the lift or Mach number.
  • Small inflow angles. φ ≈ λd/uT, with first-harmonic inflow only. No dynamic inflow, wake roll-up, vortex ring state or windmill-brake state: use descent speeds near the induced velocity with great care.
  • Tip loss is a radial cut at B for loading and momentum area; profile drag still integrates to the tip.
  • Compressibility only scales the lift slope (Prandtl-Glauert, subsonic, Madv < 1). No wave drag.
  • No ground effect, no interference, no airframe, no blade elasticity.
  • Atmosphere is ISA pressure with a typed temperature, valid in the troposphere.
